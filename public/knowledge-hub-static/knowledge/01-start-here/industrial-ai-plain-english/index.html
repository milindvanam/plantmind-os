Industrial AI in Plain English

Executive takeaway

Industrial AI is a decision-support layer above the systems that already run the plant. It combines operating signals with asset, process and work context to identify patterns worth investigating. It does not replace control systems, engineering judgment or accountable decision-makers.

What it is​

Industrial AI applies analytical and machine-learning methods to industrial work: reliability, production, energy, quality, safety and maintenance. The useful unit is not “a model.” It is a governed chain from a business question to an operating decision.

SCADA and DCS platforms monitor and control processes. Historians preserve time-series data. ERP and CMMS platforms manage equipment, work and cost. Industrial AI can learn across these sources while leaving their responsibilities intact.

A concrete example​

An induced-draft fan may show rising bearing vibration. A threshold alarm can report the exceedance. A contextual analytical workflow can also consider load, speed, bearing temperature, lubrication history and recent work orders before presenting a finding. The maintenance engineer still decides whether to inspect, monitor or plan intervention.

Where value comes from​

Value arises when earlier or clearer information changes a decision: inspection timing, planned work, process set-point review, energy-loss investigation or quality intervention. A model score has no value until it changes a workflow safely and the result is measured.

What it cannot do by itself​

  • Repair poor instrumentation, missing timestamps or inconsistent asset identifiers.
  • Establish causality from correlation alone.
  • Safely automate a critical action without authority boundaries and engineering review.
  • Guarantee savings before a baseline and measurement method exist.
